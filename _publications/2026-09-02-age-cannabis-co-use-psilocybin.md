This retrospective study examined how age and cannabis co-use relate to psilocybin experiences, perceived psychological benefits, and broader substance co-use patterns among adults who currently use psilocybin.

Participants were grouped by age, allowing the study to compare acute experience profiles and perceived outcomes across younger, middle-aged, and older adults. The findings suggest that age may shape aspects of the acute psilocybin experience, while cannabis co-use was associated with differences in perceived benefits across several domains.

The study contributes to a growing real-world literature on psychedelic use outside clinical settings, with implications for public health, harm reduction, and future research on individual differences in psychedelic experience.
